# Favorite Android Apps

Android apps I actually use and recommend, with a line on why where I had one. Picked from my phone and my Google Play library; apps since removed from the Play Store are left out.

The same list as data: [`exports/2026-11-16/apps.json`](exports/2026-11-16/apps.json) (name, package, category, source, link, comment). Each new review adds a dated export.

## Audio & voice

- **[ASR Voice Recorder](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nll.asr)**: My go-to voice recorder. I use it constantly, for everything from voice-note prompts for AI tools to podcast episodes. I really appreciate how easy it makes setting up different recording profiles, with precise controls for each use case.
- **[Easy Voice Recorder Pro](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.digipom.easyvoicerecorder.pro)**: I do so much voice work that I like to keep a second recorder on hand, and Easy Voice Recorder Pro is also excellent.
- **[USB Audio Recorder Pro](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.extreamsd.usbaudiorecorderpro)**
- **[Spectrum](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.intoorbit.spectrum)**: Having lived in far too many noisy apartments next to building sites and other noise nuisances, I like to keep at least one app on my phone for measuring noise and figuring out which frequencies are coming in. A simple spectrum analysis on an Android phone can often give you a head start on blocking a nuisance sound. This one is reliable and great.
- **[phyphox](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=de.rwth_aachen.phyphox)**: Absolutely outstanding: it exposes the phone's sensors. I've used it not only to figure out which frequencies were coming in from a noisy construction site, but even to use the gyroscope to work out how much of the sound was travelling as vibration. Mind-blowing that a consumer Android phone can do this.
- **[Wispr Flow](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.wispr.flowapp)**
- **[Whisperian](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.whisperian.client)**
- **[Ramblr](https://f-droid.org/packages/com.trevornk.ramblr/)**
- **[Kokoro TTS](https://f-droid.org/packages/dev.kokoro.tts/)**: My favourite engine for on-device TTS.
- **[SpeakThat](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.micoyc.speakthat)**: This became indispensable during the protracted war with Iran here in Israel, when we were often woken in the middle of the night by alerts. Having them read out in a calm, sometimes fun voice became a coping mechanism for the stress. Generally useful, and it fits my preference for using voice for just about everything, including receiving notifications.
- **[Podcast Addict](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bambuna.podcastaddict)**: The Pro version of Podcast Addict is my go-to podcast client. Very happy with it.
- **[Voice (audiobook player)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=de.ph1b.audiobook)**
- **[SoundCloud: Music & Audio](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.soundcloud.android)**
- **[Suno - AI Music & Songs Maker](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.suno.android)**
- **[User Dictionary Manager (UDM)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.usr.dict.mgr)**
- **[Voicenotes](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.app.voicenotes)**

## Camera & scanning

- **[Open Camera](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.sourceforge.opencamera)**
- **[Timestamp Camera](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jeyluta.timestampcamera)**: Extremely versatile and useful in so many situations: evidence for tenancy and landlord issues, templates for recording inventory, labelling network gear during tests, photographing PCBs when opening up components.
- **[Genius Scan](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.thegrizzlylabs.geniusscan.free)**: My favourite scanning tool, and a cornerstone of my lifelong effort to minimise paper and digitise whatever possible. - **[Binary Eye](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=de.markusfisch.android.binaryeye)**: I always keep a QR code scanner as part of my home inventory system, and this is the one I like best.
